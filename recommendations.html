My pick: B, with A’s project layout

Keep the film. Make the offer clearer. Put the work where people can see it.

B best reflects the supplied branding brief’s mix of condensed type and italic contrast. Its opening explains the breadth of Triple Down’s offer. A’s equal project columns are easier to scan than B’s staggered arrangement. I would combine those two and keep the site predominantly black and white.

What to add

  1. A clearer introduction. Keep the original footage and add a short explanation of the hospitality and events offer, with routes into the work and a conversation.
  2. Visible projects. Bring three existing projects onto the homepage. Each shows the brand, the work involved and a link to the existing case study. This makes Triple Down’s range easier to understand without opening the menu.
  3. A proper contact finish. Turn the existing email address into a clear invitation to discuss a venue, event or brand, with a working email link.

The three versions

A — closest to current. Existing headline, condensed typography and monochrome styling. Adds clarity, project links and contact without changing the overall visual language.

B — editorial. Uses “Hospitality isn’t one department. Neither are we.” from the branding brief, with italic serif contrast and a more editorial project layout.

C — colour accents. Keeps the current headline and adds yellow-green and pink accents inspired by the supplied references. This tests a more expressive direction; the colours are proposals, not an approved brand palette.

Already changed

The logo strip now keeps moving when hovered. The pause button has been removed. The device’s reduced-motion setting is still respected. These fixes apply to the current preview and all three variants.

What stays

The existing photography, video streams, typography foundation, seven project pages and the rest of the homepage remain. The variants are separate review pages; none has replaced the current preview or the live Triple Down domain.

The project summaries are grounded in the published Beastwang, Doppler and Audrey pages. No performance figures or testimonials have been invented.

After choosing a direction

Add two or three specific client outcomes once the figures and wording are confirmed. A short account of the problem, Triple Down’s role and the result would strengthen the case studies more than another decorative section.
