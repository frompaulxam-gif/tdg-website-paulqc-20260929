What I would leave out

Countdowns, scarcity banners, aggressive loss framing, invented pricing tiers, unsupported guarantees and extra pop-ups. They would introduce claims or pressure that the current brief does not justify. Before/after imagery is only useful when the pair genuinely demonstrates the same project’s change; unrelated photos should not be presented as measured proof.

How strong is the evidence?

The reels show design reasoning, not an A/B test or measured conversion uplift. Their universal conversion promises go beyond the evidence shown. Nielsen Norman Group’s navigation study supports making important routes visible, but it measured usability on other sites, not enquiries for Triple Down. On mobile, it compared hidden and partly visible navigation rather than a fully visible menu.

My recommendation is therefore a design hypothesis: make the offer easier to understand and the next step easier to find. After a direction is chosen, evaluate actual project enquiries and their quality. PaulQC establishes visual preference; it does not establish conversion performance.
